============================================================
B. PRODUCT PRINCIPLES
============================================================

1. VOICE FIRST
The primary training experience must be a natural spoken conversation with an AI coach. Text entry is an optional alternative, not the default learning experience.

2. PERSONALIZED
The user's function, role, seniority, audience, goals, observed difficulties, and learning history must influence the exercises.

3. DIAGNOSTIC BEFORE PRESCRIPTIVE
Do not prescribe generic communication training without first investigating the user's actual needs.

4. EVIDENCE-BASED FEEDBACK
Feedback must refer to observable details of the user's response. Do not fabricate mistakes or infer unsupported psychological explanations.

5. ONE PRIORITY AT A TIME
Give one or two targeted improvements per exercise instead of overwhelming the learner.

6. LEARN THROUGH PRACTICE
Provide hints and examples when needed, but ensure users also attempt comparable tasks independently.

7. MEASURE TRANSFER
Improvement means performing better in new scenarios, not memorizing sample answers.

8. PROFESSIONAL BUT ENGAGING
The UI must be clean, premium, intuitive, animated, and encouraging without feeling childish.

9. PRIVACY BY DESIGN
A learner's recordings and personal coaching feedback must remain private by default.

10. COMMERCIAL VIABILITY
AI voice usage, infrastructure cost, and subscription entitlements must be measured and controlled from the first release.

D. THREE SUBSCRIPTION MODULES
============================================================

MODULE 1: DAILY COMMUNICATION

Target audience:
Individual contributors, early-career professionals, job seekers, and anyone who wants stronger spoken communication.

Learning objectives:
- Start conversations comfortably.
- Select the first thought to communicate.
- Translate thoughts into words.
- Construct complete sentences.
- Retrieve or paraphrase words.
- Reduce unnecessary sentence restarts.
- Improve clear, natural expression.
- Explain ideas in a logical sequence.
- Respond spontaneously.
- Participate in workplace discussions.
- Improve intelligibility and spoken delivery.

Framework:
TASC:
Think -> Anchor -> Speak -> Continue.

Progressive exercises:
1. Convert a provided thought into a sentence.
2. Independently select an opening sentence.
3. Explain one idea in three sentences.
4. Respond to unexpected questions.
5. Maintain a short natural conversation.
6. Recover after losing a train of thought.
7. Explain an unfamiliar scenario without relying on a script.

Do not interpret all pauses or sentence restarts as defects. Evaluate their impact on the communication task.

MODULE 2: MANAGEMENT & BUSINESS COMMUNICATION

Target audience:
People managers, HR professionals, engineering managers, product managers, finance managers, project managers, sales managers, and other business-facing professionals.

Learning objectives:
- Convert data into meaningful business information.
- Identify the central message.
- Distinguish observed facts from interpretations.
- Explain specific business implications.
- Make evidence-based recommendations.
- Give and receive feedback.
- Delegate and clarify expectations.
- Explain business decisions.
- Manage stakeholders.
- Respond to objections.
- Conduct difficult conversations.
- Provide executive updates.
- Communicate risks and trade-offs.

Frameworks:
DIMA:
Data -> Information -> Meaning -> Action.

CLEAR:
Context -> Listen -> Explain -> Align -> Review.

Include simulations involving:
- Leadership meetings.
- Project reviews.
- Employee feedback.
- Performance management.
- Stakeholder conflicts.
- Resource constraints.
- Business risk discussions.
- Escalations.
- Change management.

MODULE 3: LEADERSHIP & PUBLIC SPEAKING

Target audience:
Executives, senior leaders, entrepreneurs, founders, and aspiring leaders.

Learning objectives:
- Executive communication.
- Public speaking.
- Storytelling.
- Strategic presentations.
- Vision communication.
- Audience engagement.
- Investor and business pitches.
- Explaining complex ideas simply.
- Handling challenging questions.
- Opening and concluding presentations.
- Speaking spontaneously.
- Communicating through uncertainty.
- Facilitating group discussions.

Framework:
MESSAGE:
Main message -> Evidence -> Significance -> Story -> Audience -> Goal -> Ending.

============================================================

Create a diagnostic system that evaluates several independent communication dimensions.

DIMENSIONS:

1. Conversation initiation.
2. Thought selection and organization.
3. Sentence construction.
4. Word retrieval and paraphrasing.
5. Information synthesis.
6. Evidence-based reasoning.
7. Business implications and recommendations.
8. Conversational responsiveness.
9. Spoken intelligibility and delivery.
10. Audience-appropriate communication.

The system must differentiate:
- A user-reported difficulty.
- An observed communication behavior.
- A possible explanation requiring more evidence.
- A confirmed learning need supported by repeated observations.

Do not diagnose medical, psychological, or cognitive conditions.

Do not infer intelligence, personality, employability, or professional fitness from speech.

INITIAL DIAGNOSTIC:

Conduct short voice exercises:

1. Introduce yourself or explain your professional role.
2. Start a conversation about a familiar topic.
3. Explain a provided idea in one sentence.
4. Answer an open-ended question without a provided framework.
5. Interpret a small set of role-relevant facts.
6. Respond to a challenging follow-up.
7. Complete a short speech-delivery task.

Use controlled diagnostic probes.

Example:

Observation:
A user repeatedly restarts sentences during an open-ended business question.

Probe A:
Provide the central thought and ask the user to express it.

Probe B:
Remove the central thought and ask a comparable question.

Probe C:
Provide a short opening-sentence anchor.

Probe D:
Introduce a new comparable question without the anchor.

Compare results before assigning a primary development need.

Store:
- Assessment date.
- Scenario and version.
- Transcript.
- Relevant audio metadata when available.
- User-reported experience.
- Observable evidence.
- Assessed skill dimensions.
- Confidence or uncertainty.
- Recommended next exercise.
- Rubric and model versions.

Do not assign an arbitrary overall score based on one short answer.

J. PERSONALIZED COACHING ENGINE
============================================================

The coaching system must behave like an experienced professional communication trainer.

It should:
- Ask one question at a time.
- Let the user finish their thought.
- Avoid unnecessary interruptions.
- Avoid excessive praise.
- Give specific and constructive feedback.
- Identify no more than one or two priority improvements per attempt.
- Explain improvements using examples from the user's response.
- Adapt to the user's demonstrated skill level.
- Ask follow-up questions that test comprehension and real-world application.
- Avoid repeating exercises the user has already mastered.

Each training cycle:

1. Introduce the objective.
2. Ask a role-relevant question.
3. Capture the user's natural response.
4. Evaluate the response.
5. Identify one priority development area.
6. Give concise feedback.
7. Provide a hint or example when necessary.
8. Ask the user to retry independently.
9. Introduce a new comparable scenario.
10. Update the learning profile based on repeated evidence.

EXAMPLE:

Scenario:
A manager explains why a product release is delayed.

User response:
A long description of multiple issues without identifying the main cause.

AI feedback:
"You explained several dependencies, but the main cause of the delay was not clear. State the principal cause in one sentence."

Follow-up:
"Now explain the impact on the launch date."

Transfer exercise:
Introduce a different project-delay scenario and assess whether the user can communicate the cause and impact independently.

Do not simply show a perfect script and reward the user for reading it.
